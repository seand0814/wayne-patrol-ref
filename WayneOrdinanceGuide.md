# Wayne Township Ordinance Field Guide for Patrol

Code current through legislation of 06-17-2026 (eCode360 download) · Prepared Nov 16, 2026 · @SEAN

## How to use this guide

Every section number here was checked against the Township of Wayne General Code (eCode360 download dated 11-16-2026, which covers legislation through 06-17-2026). Ordinances amended after that date are not reflected. Before an unusual or contested charge, confirm the section on eCode360 or with the municipal prosecutor.

- **Citation format:** Write it on the complaint-summons as *Wayne Code § 121-9F*: chapter, section, and subsection letter or number.
- **Penalty:** Many sections refer to the general penalty in § 1-15 (see *General Penalty Provision* near the end). Where a chapter sets its own fine, that fine is listed.
- **Who enforces:** Each entry shows the enforcing authority the code names. If the code names someone else (for example, the Construction Official or Health Officer), patrol should document the condition and refer it rather than sign the complaint, unless the code also names police.
- **Noise authority:** § 123-5 designates the Chief of Police or his/her designee as Noise Control Investigators (NCIs). Patrol can enforce noise rules based on *plainly audible*, time of day, or distance, with no sound meter. Rules that need a meter reading (decibel tables) belong to a certified Noise Control Officer.
- **"State overlap — verify"** marks a related NJ statute. It is not part of the Wayne code. Confirm the state charge separately before using it.

## Noise

Patrol enforces noise as Noise Control Investigators under § 123-5, using plainly audible, time of day and distance. Chapter 121 citations go through the NOV/NOPA process in § 121-11. § 123-4 and § 123-4.1 go to court under § 1-15.

**§ 121-11 process (applies to every Chapter 121 entry):** A *minor* violation gets a Notice of Violation (NOV) with a grace period of up to 90 days. A *non-minor* violation gets a Notice of Penalty Assessment (NOPA) if it is not corrected immediately. If it is corrected, an NOV is still issued. A repeat within 12 months gets a NOPA either way. Civil penalty: up to $2,000 per offense, with each day a separate offense (§ 121-11B). The recipient may contest a NOPA in municipal court (§ 121-11G).

### Disturbing the peace — Wayne Code § 123-4

- **Prohibits:** Unnecessary noise from a voice or a sound device that is plainly audible and excessive across a real property line and unreasonably disturbs a resident. Applies on premises the person controls or on a public street.
- **Elements:** Plainly audible across the property line; excessive; unreasonably disturbs a resident. The code defines "unusual time" to include "10:00 p.m. and 7:00 a.m. on weekdays" and 11 p.m.–9 a.m. on weekends.
- **Penalty:** § 1-15 (per § 123-6).
- **Enforced by:** Chief of Police or designee (§ 123-5).
- **Warning first:** Not required by the code.
- **Officer tip:** Note where you stood, the time, and what you heard from the complainant's side of the line.
- **State overlap — verify:** N.J.S.A. 2C:33-2 (disorderly conduct).

### Portable music in public — Wayne Code § 121-9F

- **Prohibits:** Portable, non-vehicle music or sound devices on a public space or right-of-way that are plainly audible beyond a set distance.
- **Elements:** Plainly audible at 50 ft from the operator (8 a.m.–10 p.m.) or 25 ft (10 p.m.–8 a.m.).
- **Penalty:** § 121-11 (up to $2,000).
- **Enforced by:** NCO/NCI; police as NCIs (§ 123-5).
- **Warning first:** NOV if minor; NOPA if non-minor and not corrected (§ 121-11D–E).
- **Officer tip:** Pace off or estimate the distance and record it.

### Vehicle stereos — Wayne Code § 121-10C and § 121-10D

- **Prohibits:** Vehicle music equipment that is plainly audible beyond a set distance.
- **Elements:** § 121-10C: plainly audible at 25 ft, 10 p.m.–8 a.m. § 121-10D: plainly audible at 50 ft, 8 a.m.–10 p.m. The code says the rhythmic bass alone is enough for "plainly audible" (§ 121-2).
- **Penalty:** § 121-11 (up to $2,000).
- **Enforced by:** Police as NCIs.
- **Warning first:** No grace period. § 121-10 violations are deemed purposeful and therefore non-minor.
- **Officer tip:** You don't need to identify the song or the artist.

### Modified exhaust and motorcycle mufflers — Wayne Code § 121-10A and § 121-10B

- **Prohibits:** (A) Removing or disabling a vehicle's factory noise-control equipment, or driving a vehicle modified that way. (B) Running a motorcycle without a muffler labeled under federal rule 40 CFR Part 205.
- **Elements:** The vehicle is on a public space or right-of-way, moving or stationary. Maintenance or repair is exempt.
- **Penalty:** § 121-11 (up to $2,000). Non-minor by definition.
- **Enforced by:** Police as NCIs.
- **Warning first:** No.
- **State overlap — verify:** N.J.S.A. 39:3-70 (mufflers).

### Residential power tools and yard equipment — Wayne Code § 121-9A

- **Prohibits:** A homeowner or tenant running power tools, home maintenance or yard equipment between 8:00 p.m. and 8:00 a.m., unless the meter limits are met. Motorized equipment must have a muffler.
- **Elements:** Time; the user is the owner or tenant; not emergency work.
- **Penalty:** § 121-11.
- **Enforced by:** Police as NCIs.
- **Warning first:** NOV if minor.

### Commercial landscaping — Wayne Code § 121-9B

- **Prohibits:** Commercial operators or public employees using power tools or landscaping equipment 6 p.m.–8 a.m. on weekdays, or 6 p.m.–9 a.m. on weekends and federal holidays.
- **Elements:** Time; nonresidential operator; not emergency work.
- **Penalty:** § 121-11.
- **Enforced by:** Police as NCIs.
- **Warning first:** NOV if minor.
- **Officer tip:** "Weekday" runs from Monday 7 a.m. to Friday 6 p.m. (§ 121-2).

### Construction and demolition hours — Wayne Code § 121-9C

- **Prohibits:** Construction or demolition 6 p.m.–7 a.m. on weekdays, or 6 p.m.–9 a.m. on weekends and federal holidays.
- **Elements:** Time; construction or demolition activity; not emergency work.
- **Penalty:** § 121-11.
- **Enforced by:** Police as NCIs.
- **Warning first:** NOV if minor.

### Burglar alarms (building or vehicle) — Wayne Code § 121-9E

- **Prohibits:** An alarm that does not shut off within 5 minutes (continuous sound) or 15 minutes (intermittent).
- **Penalty:** § 121-11. A separate charge under § 36-4B covers outdoor building alarms (see *Alarms*).
- **Enforced by:** Police as NCIs.

### Animal noise — Wayne Code § 121-9G (also § 68-14)

- **Prohibits:** An owner or tenant letting an animal make sound across a property line that unreasonably disturbs a resident, or refusing to stop it when an NCO/NCI orders.
- **Elements:** Prima facie: vocalizing 5 minutes without interruption (average 4 or more per minute), or 20 minutes intermittently (average 2 or more per minute). § 68-14 uses the same thresholds for dogs.
- **Penalty:** § 121-11. § 68-14 carries the § 1-15 penalty (§ 68-6.1B).
- **Enforced by:** Police as NCIs; animal control officers and police for Chapter 68 (§ 68-15).
- **Defense:** It is an affirmative defense that the animal was intentionally provoked.
- **Officer tip:** Time the barking yourself and log the count.

### Engine compression (jake) brakes — Wayne Code § 123-4.1

- **Prohibits:** Using an engine compression brake that makes excessive, loud noise on these roads: Alps Rd (Maple Ave to Rt 23), Central Ave, Dorsa Ave, French Hill Rd (Alps Rd to Parish Dr), Haul Rd, Maple Ave, Oldham Rd, Parish Dr, Preakness Ave, Ratzer Rd, Ryerson Ave and Whitmore Ave.
- **Exempt:** Public safety vehicles, and emergencies where the driver reasonably believes the brake is needed to protect people or property.
- **Penalty:** § 1-15 (per § 123-6).
- **Enforced by:** Chief of Police or designee (§ 123-5).

**Meter-only rules (refer to a certified NCO):** § 121-7 sets decibel limits by property type and time, for example 65 dBA by day and 50 dBA at night at a residential line. § 121-8 limits indoor dB(C) increases from sound devices. Both require a sound level meter.

## Parking, Traffic and Vehicles

Chapter 200 violations carry a fine of up to $75, up to 15 days, or both (§ 200-3), unless a section below says otherwise. Street-by-street restrictions are in the schedules at §§ 200-29 to 200-48.1.

### Overnight parking (2 a.m.–6 a.m.) — Wayne Code § 200-5B

- **Prohibits:** Parking on any street between 2:00 a.m. and 6:00 a.m.
- **Exempt streets (§ 200-29C):** Atherton Ct (from Valley Rd to the Building 8 and 11 entrances), Byrne Ct (west side), Knox Terrace (north side), Lancaster Ct, Manchester Ct, Old Newark Pompton Tpk, Royal Ct, Salisbury Rd and Traphagen Rd.
- **Penalty:** § 200-3 (up to $75).
- **Enforced by:** Police.
- **Warning first:** No.

### Posted restrictions (no parking, time limits, no stopping) — Wayne Code §§ 200-5A, 200-6, 200-7, 200-8

- **Prohibits:** Parking against a scheduled restriction: no parking at any time (§ 200-5A, Schedule I), during set hours (§ 200-6, Schedule II), stopping or standing (§ 200-7, Schedule III), or past a time limit (§ 200-8, Schedule IV).
- **Elements:** The street and side are in the schedule; the time window applies.
- **Penalty:** § 200-3.
- **Officer tip:** Check that the street is listed in the schedule before writing. Cite the operative section (for example, § 200-6) and note the schedule.
- **State overlap — verify:** N.J.S.A. 39:4-138 (statewide no-parking zones such as crosswalks, driveways and intersections).

### Snow on the roadway — Wayne Code § 200-22

- **Prohibits:** Parking on any street or highway while snow covers the streets, until plowing is done enough that parking won't block traffic.
- **Penalty:** § 200-3. The vehicle may be towed at the owner's cost (§ 200-24).
- **Enforced by:** Police; "any peace officer" may have the vehicle removed (§ 200-24).
- **Caution:** § 200-25 makes this article effective only where signs are posted as required by law. Confirm signage before charging.

### Emergency no-parking — Wayne Code § 200-23

- **Prohibits:** Parking where temporary emergency no-parking signs are posted after an emergency is declared.
- **Elements:** The Chief, or in his absence the ranking officer, declared the emergency (weather, crashes, fires, celebrations). Owners of vehicles parked before the signs went up must be notified (§ 200-23B).
- **Penalty:** § 200-3; towing under § 200-24.

### Hydrants and fire department connections — Wayne Code § 205-38B

- **Prohibits:** Standing or parking within 10 ft of a fire hydrant or fire department connection. Applies on public, semipublic or private property and roadways.
- **Penalty:** Fine up to $50, up to 15 days, or both. The vehicle is towed at the owner's expense.
- **Enforced by:** Police.
- **Officer tip:** This section covers private lots, too.
- **State overlap — verify:** N.J.S.A. 39:4-138(d).

### Handicapped parking (off-street public and semipublic lots) — Wayne Code § 200-10.2

- **Prohibits:** Standing or parking in a posted disabled space without the MVC placard, plate or insignia. The placard goes in the lower right corner of the windshield (§ 200-10.3). Damaging a disabled-parking sign is a separate violation (§ 200-10.4).
- **Penalty:** $250 first offense; at least $250 and up to 90 days community service after that (§ 200-10.5).
- **Enforced by:** Police.
- **State overlap — verify:** N.J.S.A. 39:4-197 and 39:4-207.9.

### Private lots under Title 39 (malls, shopping centers, schools, condos) — Wayne Code § 200-10

- **What it does:** Applies Title 39 and the listed traffic controls on private properties whose owners asked for it. The properties are listed in Schedules VI, VIA, VIB, VIC and VII (§§ 200-34 to 200-35.70), including Willowbrook Mall, Wayne Towne Centre and the high schools.
- **Officer tip:** Title 39 enforcement on a private lot is valid only if the property is listed. Check the schedule first.

### Truck exclusions (over 4 tons) — Wayne Code § 200-11

- **Prohibits:** Trucks over 4 tons gross weight on the streets in Schedule VIII (§ 200-36), except for pickup and delivery on those streets.
- **Penalty:** § 200-3.

### Boats, campers and trailers in residential driveways — Wayne Code § 134-67.1A

- **Prohibits:** (1) Storing boats, commercial or recreational trailers, mobile homes or RVs in a front-yard driveway. They may go in a side or rear yard at least 5 ft from the side line and 10 ft from the rear line. (2) Parking vehicles anywhere other than a paved driveway; no parking on grass.
- **Penalty:** Up to $1,250 or 90 days (§ 134-122). Each day after 10 days' written notice is a separate offense.
- **Enforced by:** Zoning Officer / Construction Official. Refer it.

### Commercial vehicles in residential zones — Wayne Code § 134-67.1B

- **Rule:** No more than one commercial vehicle, school bus or limousine parked overnight. It must weigh no more than 15,000 lbs GVWR, be registered to the occupant or the occupant's employer, and sit on a paved surface. It may be no more than 84 in. wide or high and no more than 240 in. long.
- **Penalty:** § 134-122.
- **Enforced by:** Zoning Officer. Refer it.

### Abandoned and junk vehicles — Wayne Code § 198-3 and § 198-4

- **Prohibits:** (§ 198-3) Leaving an abandoned vehicle outdoors on public or private land or a street. "Abandoned" means no current registration, or so disabled that it obstructs traffic and the owner won't move it. (§ 198-4) Keeping a junk automobile on any property.
- **Exempt:** Vehicles in a garage or building, lawful junkyards, vehicles held at a lawful repair or sales business, and unregistered vehicles used only on private property.
- **Penalty:** § 1-15 (§ 198-5).
- **Enforced by:** Police impound abandoned or stolen vehicles; fees are under Chapter 191 (§ 198-3.1). For junk autos, the violation begins after 10 days' notice from the Property Maintenance Official.
- **State overlap — verify:** N.J.S.A. 39:4-56.5 et seq.

### Roll-off dumpsters — Wayne Code § 83-10 and § 83-11

- **Prohibits:** (§ 83-10) A roll-off dumpster on a municipal road or property without a Construction Official permit, which is valid for no more than 30 days. In the road it needs 18 in. x 18 in. reflective diamond panels at both ends, mounted at least 3 ft high, and reflective cones around it. (§ 83-11) A dumpster on private property without a permit; more than 48 hours without an active construction permit; or within 10 ft of a property line unless fully in a paved driveway.
- **Penalty:** § 83-12: $500–$10,000, with each day a separate violation (Article I). § 83-11E points to § 1-15.
- **Enforced by:** Construction Official; the Chief of Police or designee reviews street placements (§ 83-10D).

### Road work zones — Wayne Code §§ 200-52 to 200-60

- **Rules:** Work in the roadway must follow the MUTCD (§ 200-52B). Contractors must hold a preconstruction meeting with the Traffic Bureau (§ 200-53). No work on Traffic Bureau-listed heavily traveled roads before 9 a.m. or after 4 p.m. (§ 200-56). Road closings and detours need Traffic Bureau approval (§ 200-57). Intersections with signals need a police officer, not a civilian flagger (§ 200-58C).
- **Penalty:** § 1-15 (§ 200-61).
- **Enforced by:** The Wayne PD Traffic Bureau can stop work (§ 200-59).

## Solicitation, Canvassing and Peddling

Every Chapter 60 violation carries the same fine: $100 for a first offense, $500 for a second and $1,000 for a third (§ 60-11A). The Chief may also suspend a permit pending a revocation hearing (§ 60-8C).

### Permit required — Wayne Code § 60-2

- **Prohibits:** Commercial canvassing, soliciting in a public place, going house to house, selling by sample, or taking orders for goods or services without a written permit from the Chief of Police.
- **Exempt from the permit only (§ 60-1B):** Delivery of ordered goods; holders of a state or county license who carry employer ID; resident honorably discharged veterans or exempt firefighters with a state peddler's license (N.J.S.A. 45:24-9 and 45:24-10); political campaigning; noncommercial canvassing.
- **Penalty:** § 60-11.
- **Enforced by:** Police (Records and Investigation Bureau issues permits).
- **Warning first:** No.
- **Officer tip:** Permits expire December 31 (§ 60-3). The permit carries the holder's photo and must be worn where the public can see it (§ 60-5B).

### Conduct rules (apply to everyone, permitted or exempt) — Wayne Code § 60-7

- **§ 60-7A:** Circulars and samples must be handed to an occupant or left in a secure place.
- **§ 60-7B:** No entering a home without the occupant's express invitation.
- **§ 60-7C:** No claiming exclusive rights to a street location or blocking the public in congested areas.
- **§ 60-7D:** No soliciting where a sign prohibits it.
- **§ 60-7E:** No littering streets or property with merchandise or printed material.
- **§ 60-7F:** No soliciting on a public roadway or right-of-way, or from a vehicle in the roadway.
- **§ 60-7G:** No soliciting between 7:00 p.m. and 9:00 a.m.
- **§ 60-7H:** Licensees must carry the license, show it to any resident on request, and display ID while soliciting.
- **Penalty:** § 60-11.
- **State overlap — verify:** Applying the hours limit (§ 60-7G) to noncommercial, political or religious canvassers raises First Amendment issues. Check with the prosecutor before charging them.

### Nonsolicitation list — Wayne Code § 60-10

- **Prohibits:** A permit holder canvassing, soliciting or peddling at any address on the Township Clerk's nonsolicitation list (§ 60-10B).
- **Elements:** The address was on the list given to the permittee. The Clerk updates it quarterly on Jan 1, Apr 1, Jul 1 and Oct 1.
- **Penalty:** § 60-11.
- **Public education:** Residents sign up on a form at the Township Clerk's office (§ 60-10A).

### Handbills and flyers

See *Littering* below: flyers on driveways (§ 112-4B–E) and on vehicles (§ 112-11).

## Littering, Dumping, Refuse and Recycling

Most Chapter 112 violations are punished as unlawful solid waste disposal: a minimum fine of $2,500 and a maximum of $10,000, with each day a separate violation, plus the Township's cleanup cost (§ 112-16C; § 1-15.1B and § 1-15.2B). Chapter 112 does not name a police enforcer except where noted, so confirm department practice on who signs.

### Illegal dumping — Wayne Code § 112-14

- **Prohibits:** (A) Dumping solid waste along any street or road, on or off the right-of-way, or in any body of water. (B) Dumping on any public or private property without the owner's written consent, and then only in a proper container. Covers household, commercial and industrial waste; appliances, furniture, vehicles, parts and tires; construction debris; and leaves, brush and tree waste.
- **Penalty:** $2,500–$10,000 per day, plus cleanup cost (§ 112-16C).
- **Warning first:** No.
- **Officer tip:** Photograph the debris and look for addressed mail or receipts in it. Note any vehicle or plate seen.
- **State overlap — verify:** N.J.S.A. 13:1E-9.3 (illegal dumping) and N.J.S.A. 2C:17-2.

### Littering from a vehicle — Wayne Code § 112-10

- **Prohibits:** A driver or passenger throwing or depositing litter on a street, other public place or private property.
- **Penalty:** § 112-16C ($2,500–$10,000).
- **State overlap — verify:** N.J.S.A. 39:4-63 and 39:4-64 (throwing litter from a vehicle).

### Litter on public, vacant or private property — Wayne Code §§ 112-2, 112-12, 112-4A

- **§ 112-2:** No placing anything on a street, public place, public parking lot or public building that blocks it or makes it unsightly. Litter in public cans must be secured against wind.
- **§ 112-12:** No throwing litter on any open or vacant property, public or private.
- **§ 112-4A:** No leaving litter or bulk items (appliances, furniture, mattresses, tires) outside on private property, except in an enclosed structure or on bulk collection days.
- **Penalty:** § 112-16C.
- **Enforced by:** The Health Officer or designee abates and liens after 5 days' notice (§ 112-16A).

### Unsolicited flyers on property — Wayne Code § 112-4B to § 112-4E

- **Rule:** Unsolicited flyers or shoppers must go in a receptacle meant for them. If there is none, they go on the ground under the mailbox or within 3 ft of it. Delivery must stop once the owner has sent a certified-mail stop request.
- **Penalty:** The code is inconsistent. § 112-4F says up to $100 per delivery, while § 112-16C says up to $500 per improper distribution. Confirm with the prosecutor.

### Handbills on vehicles — Wayne Code § 112-11

- **Prohibits:** Placing any commercial or noncommercial handbill on a vehicle. It is allowed to hand a free noncommercial handbill to a willing occupant in a public place.
- **Penalty:** Up to $500 (§ 112-16C, printed matter).

### Loads, mud and spilled debris — Wayne Code § 112-9

- **Prohibits:** (A) Hauling stone, dirt, sand, gravel, fill, clippings or waste without a tarp. The operator or owner is liable for any spill even if the load was covered. (B) Tracking mud or debris onto a street. (C) Failing to clean up immediately after glass or debris falls from a vehicle.
- **Penalty:** § 112-16C.
- **Enforced by:** Director of Public Works or designee (§ 112-9D).
- **State overlap — verify:** N.J.S.A. 39:4-77 (spilling loads).

### Sidewalks, bins and construction sites — Wayne Code §§ 112-3, 112-7, 112-8, 112-15

- **§ 112-3:** No sweeping litter into a gutter, street or catch basin. Owners and occupants must keep sidewalks and lots in front of their property free of litter.
- **§ 112-7:** Owners and occupants must keep the abutting sidewalk and curb free of obstructions and nuisances.
- **§ 112-8:** No open or overflowing waste bins on the property.
- **§ 112-15:** Construction sites must have adequate containers and must not let litter build up.
- **Penalty:** § 112-16C.

### Garbage set-out times — Wayne Code § 83-5D

- **Rule:** Garbage goes to the curb no earlier than the day before collection and no later than 6:00 a.m. on collection day. Cans must be removed the day of collection and stored out of sight. Cans are limited to 35 gallons and 50 lbs with tight lids (§ 83-5A).
- **Penalty:** A written warning for the first violation, then § 1-15. (§ 83-12 separately lists $500–$10,000 for Article I; confirm which the prosecutor uses.)
- **Warning first:** Yes. A written warning is required.

### Recycling and yard waste set-out — Wayne Code § 141-4C and § 141-4D

- **Rule:** Recycling and yard waste go to the curb no earlier than the day before collection and by 6:00 a.m. on collection day. Containers must be removed the day of collection. Yard waste must be in paper bags or containers of 32–35 gallons. **No plastic bags, and no loose yard waste in or on the curb or street.**
- **Penalty:** § 141-4D: written warning, then § 1-15. § 141-10A: $100–$500, with one written warning to residents first.
- **Enforced by:** Director of Public Works or designee (§ 141-2).
- **Warning first:** Yes, for residents.
- **E-waste:** May not go to the curb. It goes to the Wayne Recycling Center (§ 141-4I).

### Scavenging recyclables — Wayne Code § 141-7A

- **Prohibits:** Anyone except the Township, its contractors or the owner's hauler taking or tampering with curbside recyclables, from 6:00 p.m. the day before collection for 24 hours. Once at the curb, recyclables are Township property.
- **Penalty:** $500–$1,000, with no warning required (§ 141-10B).

### Discarded refrigerators — Wayne Code § 141-6

## E-Bikes, E-Scooters, Off-Road and Low-Speed Vehicles

Chapter 202 was rewritten on 11-5-2025 (Ord. No. 61-2025). Wayne PD enforces it and may use discretion, weighing severity, circumstances, the rider's history and public safety (§ 202-6).

**Penalty for every § 202-5 violation (§ 202-7):** The penalty for Township traffic violations, or the § 1-15 penalty, with a **$100 minimum for repeat offenders**. Separately, an owner or custodian who knowingly lets an underage person operate is a separate violation (§ 202-7C). Any vehicle operated in violation may be impounded and towed under Chapter 191. For a juvenile, the owner or parent pays the tow costs (§ 202-7D).

**Definitions (§ 202-2):**

| Type | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Low-speed e-bike, Class 1 | Pedals, motor under 750 W, assists only while pedaling, cuts off at 20 mph |
| Low-speed e-bike, Class 2 | Pedals, motor under 750 W, can propel without pedaling, cuts off at 20 mph |
| Low-speed e-scooter | Floorboard and handlebars, electric, top speed under 19 mph |
| Motorized bicycle | Pedal bike with a helper motor (under 50 cc or 1.5 bhp, or electric), top speed no more than 25 mph; or an electric bike over 20 mph with a motor-powered top speed of no more than 28 mph |
| Off-road vehicle (ORV) | ATVs, dirt bikes, minibikes, go-karts, snowmobiles |
| Low-speed vehicle (LSV) | Four wheels, 20–25 mph, under 3,000 lbs GVWR |

### Off-road and low-speed vehicles — Wayne Code § 202-5A

- **(1)** No operation between 8:00 p.m. and 8:00 a.m.
- **(2)** A factory or equivalent muffler is required.
- **(3)** Not within 400 ft of an affected person's occupied property. No violation exists if the NCO or NCI finds the use is not affecting the complainant.
- **(4)** Not on another person's private property without the owner's or occupant's prior express permission.
- **(5)** Not on public grounds without permission.
- **(6)** No loud, unnecessary or unusual noise that disturbs others.
- **(7)** No careless, reckless or negligent operation.
- **(8)** **Not on any public street in Wayne.**
- **(9)** Not on Township athletic fields, courts, grass areas or natural-surface trails.
- **Exceptions (§ 202-4):** Farming, yard work and snow removal; Township and emergency use; travel to and from a road or loading for transport; mowers and lawn equipment; Township-permitted events; permitted racetracks; MSF-type safety training with Township permission; and maintenance runs 8 a.m.–8 p.m. of no more than 15 minutes an hour, twice a day.

### E-bikes, e-scooters and motorized bicycles — Wayne Code § 202-5B

- **(1)** No careless, reckless or negligent operation, including failing to yield to pedestrians.
- **(2)** Must obey traffic signals, signs and control devices, unless directed by an officer.
- **(3)** Not on Township athletic fields, courts, grass areas or natural-surface trails.
- **(4)** **E-bikes may not be ridden on a road with a speed limit over 25 mph.**
- **(5)** No passengers.
- **(6)** E-bike riders **under 17** must wear a helmet.
- **(7)** No parking an e-bike or e-scooter so that it blocks a sidewalk.
- **(8)** Motorized bicycle operators must be at least 15, licensed, registered and insured. Every motorized bicycle rider must wear a helmet, whatever their age.
- **(9)** No handheld phone or electronic device while riding. Hands-free use is allowed.
- **(10)** Front and rear reflectors are required. At night, a white front lamp and a red rear lamp, each visible at 500 ft.
- **Officer tip:** Classify the device first using the table above, since the rules differ by type. A "motorized bicycle" needs a license, registration and insurance; a Class 1 or 2 e-bike does not.
- **State overlap — verify:** N.J.S.A. 39:4-14.16 (low-speed e-bikes and e-scooters), N.J.S.A. 39:4-14.3 et seq. (motorized bicycles), N.J.S.A. 39:4-10.1 (helmets).

### Motor vehicles on school paths — Wayne Code § 180-34

- **Prohibits:** Driving any type of motor vehicle on a school path, whether on public or private property.
- **Penalty:** Article VII sets none of its own; § 1-15 applies by default.

## Property Maintenance, Sidewalks, Snow and Right-of-Way

### Sidewalk snow and ice (homes and businesses) — Wayne Code § 180-20

- **Requires:** The owner or tenant of land abutting a public-street sidewalk must clear snow and ice within **48 hours** after the snow falls or the ice forms, leaving a clear path at least **36 inches** wide.
- **Penalty:** $100 per violation (§ 180-25). The Township may clear it and lien the cost (§§ 180-23, 180-24).
- **Enforced by:** The Township Engineer, Director of Public Works **or any police officer** may issue a notice of violation or a summons (§ 180-23A).
- **Warning first:** Not required. The Township clears after the 48 hours pass.

### Plowing or blowing snow into the street — Wayne Code § 180-22

- **Prohibits:** Placing, throwing or depositing snow, ice, brush or vegetation onto the roadway between the curblines of a public street.
- **Penalty:** $100 per violation for purposefully depositing snow on the roadway (§ 180-25).
- **Enforced by:** Engineer, Director of Public Works or any police officer (§ 180-23A).
- **Public education:** This covers plow contractors and landscapers too, not just homeowners.

### Snow at apartments, condos and shopping centers — Wayne Code §§ 169-1, 169-2, 169-4

- **Requires:** Owners of multi-dwelling developments with 3 or more units (§ 169-1) and of shopping centers or commercial properties (§ 169-2) must clear private streets, walkways, lots open to the public and the area around dumpsters within **12 hours** after the storm ends. Walks must be cleared at least 36 in. wide (§ 169-4).
- **Penalty:** § 1-15 (§ 169-5). After a notice of violation expires, the Township clears it, issues a summons and liens the cost (§ 169-6).

### Unaccepted development streets — Wayne Code § 180-35

- **Requires:** The developer must clear unaccepted dedicated streets within **6 hours** after the snowfall ends, to at least **25 ft** wide. If accumulation passes 6 in., plowing must continue during the storm.

### Sidewalk vegetation — Wayne Code § 180-21

- **Requires:** Owners or tenants must keep the sidewalk area clear of vegetation that obstructs pedestrians, or at least 8 ft high where there is no sidewalk. They must clear it within **3 days** after notice.
- **Penalty:** § 180-25.

### Objects and obstructions in the street or right-of-way — Wayne Code § 180-10

- **§ 180-10I:** No permanent or temporary object or structure in the roadway or right-of-way (portable basketball hoops, rocks, posts) without Council approval. Compliant mailboxes, and refuse and cans on collection days, are allowed.
- **§ 180-10G:** No brush, hedges or objects over 2½ ft high within 10 ft of a roadway and 25 ft of an intersection if they block drivers' view.
- **§ 180-10H:** No vegetation over the roadway lower than 17 ft.
- **§ 180-10F:** No storing building materials, dirt, equipment or sheds on a street, except during road or utility work.
- **§ 180-10C–D:** No obstructing street drainage, or pumping sumps into the gutter in a way that creates an icing hazard.

- **Rules:** A permit from the Construction Official is required and must be displayed at the sale (§ 151-2A). Each sale may run up to 2 consecutive weekends, with no more than 2 permits per owner per year. Hours are **9:00 a.m.–6:00 p.m. only** (§ 151-3). Signs must be removed within 24 hours after the sale and may not go on utility poles, light standards, the right-of-way or trees (§ 151-4B–C).
- **Exempt:** The town-wide garage sale on the last two weekends of April needs no permit, but the other rules apply (§ 151-2B).
- **Penalty:** § 1-15 (§ 151-6).
- **Enforced by:** Construction Code Official (§ 151-5). Refer it.

### Temporary signs on residential property — Wayne Code § 134-68.1I(2)

- **Rules:** No permit is needed for event, sale, construction, political or advocacy signs, but each must show the name of the person or group responsible. Signs may not be lit or attached to utility poles, light stands or trees. They are limited to **12 sq ft total** per property, may go in front or side yards, and **never in the public right-of-way**. Signs in the right-of-way may be removed by the Township.
- **Political and noncommercial signs (§ 59-2):** The candidate or group files an information sheet with the Clerk before putting signs up. The designated person must remove an improper sign within **24 hours** of notice.
- **Penalty:** § 1-15 (§ 59-3); zoning penalty § 134-122.
- **Enforced by:** Zoning Officer.
- **Commercial temporary signs (§ 134-68.1I(1)):** A zoning permit is required. They may stay up no more than 30 days, are limited to 15 sq ft (banners 32 sq ft), and may not go in the right-of-way.

### Food trucks and ice cream trucks — Wayne Code §§ 149-5, 149-12, 149-15

- **Rules:** The vendor must hold a Health Officer license and post a price sign on the vehicle (§ 149-5). The license certificate must be displayed where patrons can see it (§ 149-12). An itinerant vendor may stay in one area **no more than 30 minutes a day** (§ 149-4).
- **Prohibits (§ 149-15):** Stopping to sell in a public or quasi-public place on any municipal, county or state road or right-of-way, or **within 1,000 ft of the front door** of a Township-licensed food business. Construction sites are exempt from the 1,000-ft rule.
- **Penalty:** § 1-15 (§ 149-18). The Health Officer may also suspend the license (§ 149-16).
- **Enforced by:** Health Officer licenses vendors. The code doesn't name police for this article, so document and refer.

### Landscapers — Wayne Code § 106-4

- **Requires:** (A) Company name, address and phone on both sides of every vehicle. (B) A Township decal on the front bumper and trailer, unless the company is registered under the state Home Improvement Contractor Act. (C) **Three cones at least 18 in. tall behind each parked vehicle.** (D) Tarps over yard waste in transit, and the company cleans up any spill. (E) No blowing grass or yard waste into the street and leaving it there.
- **Penalty:** § 1-15 (§ 106-8).
- **Enforced by:** **Wayne Police Department** or the Health Officer (§ 106-7).

### Secondhand gold and jewelry dealers — Wayne Code §§ 154-5, 154-6, 154-7

- **Requires:** Dealers must send the Chief a description and photos of every item bought, received or sold within **48 hours** (§ 154-5). Items must be held for **45 days** without selling or melting them and made available to police on request (§ 154-6). Dealers must ID every seller and may not deal with minors or anyone intoxicated or impaired (§ 154-7).
- **Penalty:** § 1-15 (§ 154-9). The Chief may revoke the license after a hearing (§ 154-8).

### Towing (police rotation) — Wayne Code § 191-7

- **Rules:** Only Wayne PD may call out towers and change the rotation (§ 191-7B, 7D). Towers may not cruise for business (§ 191-7E). Licensed towers must show they can guarantee a 15-minute response time (§ 191-3).
- **Penalty:** $100–$500 for a first offense; $500–$1,000 for a second within a year; at least $1,000 and a one-year license suspension for a third within a year (§ 191-16).
- **Enforced by:** Chief of Police (§ 191-15C).
- **Officer tip:** Chapter 191 does not cover tows from private property. Those fall under state rule N.J.A.C. 13:45A-31.6 (§ 191-1C).

### Shopping carts — Wayne Code §§ 162-2, 162-3, 112-13

- **Prohibits:** Taking a cart off store premises beyond the pickup area (§ 162-2), or abandoning a cart on a sidewalk, street or other property (§ 162-3). The store must mark carts with its name and "removal prohibited" and keep its lot clear (§ 112-13).
- **Penalty:** $100–$1,000 (§ 162-7). The store is held responsible and has 10 days after notice to fix the problem before a summons (§ 162-5).

## Other Quality-of-Life Ordinances

### Urinating or defecating in public — Wayne Code § 123-4.2

- **Prohibits:** Urinating or defecating on any street, sidewalk or other public or semi-public place, or anywhere in public view, except in a restroom. Also prohibited on private property outdoors without the owner's or occupant's consent.
- **Elements:** "Public view" means somewhere the act may reasonably be expected to be seen by others.
- **Penalty:** § 1-15 (§ 123-6).
- **Enforced by:** Chief of Police or designee (§ 123-5).

### Discharging firearms and weapons — Wayne Code § 77-3

- **Prohibits:** Carrying or discharging any firearm, bow and arrow, crossbow, air-driven weapon (BB or pellet gun), slingshot or other weapon in the Township, except as § 77-4 allows.
- **Exceptions (§ 77-4):** Supervised ranges; law enforcement and Fish and Wildlife on duty; defense of self, family, guests or property; ceremonial salutes (**not** holiday celebration shooting); state depredation permit holders; and unloaded transport under N.J.S.A. 2C:39-6.
- **Penalty:** § 1-15 (§ 77-11).
- **State overlap — verify:** Firearm *carry* is governed by state law (N.J.S.A. 2C:39-5 and 2C:58-4). Use § 77-3 for **discharge** complaints, such as backyard BB guns or archery, and confirm with the prosecutor before charging any carry offense under this local section.

### Short-term rentals (29 days or less) — Wayne Code § 94-23 and § 94-24

- **Prohibits:** Renting a dwelling or its land for payment for **29 days or less** (§ 94-23A), and advertising such a rental (§ 94-24). Adopted 3-4-2026.
- **Exempt:** Licensed hotels and motels; state-protected group homes; rentals of 30 days or more; and guests who pay nothing (§ 94-23B).
- **Penalty:** Civil penalty under § 1-15, regardless of intent or knowledge. It is also declared a nuisance per se (§ 94-25B–C).
- **Enforced by:** **Police Department**, Building Code Official, Fire Official, Health Department and others (§ 94-25A).
- **Officer tip:** At party or disturbance calls, ask whether the occupants are short-term renters and document the platform listing.

### Lawn watering days — Wayne Code § 205-34

- **Rule:** On Township water, **even-numbered addresses** may water lawns and landscaping Monday, Wednesday and Friday. **Odd-numbered addresses** may water Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday. On Sundays, only plants and shrubs may be watered, at any address.
- **Exempt:** New plantings for 30 days, farms, nurseries and athletic fields. Car washing, power washing, fountains and pool filling are not covered.
- **Penalty:** § 1-15 (§ 205-52A).
- **Enforced by:** Division of Water and Sewers.

### Boats on Township waters — Wayne Code §§ 53-1 to 53-7

- **Rules:** A muffler is required (§ 53-1). Motors may be no more than 10 hp, inboard or outboard, except police and municipal boats (§§ 53-2, 53-3). No loud noise, profanity, shouting or disturbing the peace from a boat (§ 53-4). **Speed limit 10 mph** (§ 53-5). Motorboats may run from one hour before sunrise to one hour after sunset on weekdays, and from 12:00 noon to one hour after sunset on Sundays (§ 53-6). No motors in the listed Ramapo River areas, no boating on floodwaters outside normal channels except for emergencies or access to one's own property, and no wakes that wash floodwater against buildings (§ 53-7).
- **Penalty:** § 1-15 (§ 53-8).

### Swimming facilities — Wayne Code § 129-3 and § 129-3.1

- **Rules:** No entering or using Township swim facilities outside posted days and hours (§ 129-3). No swimming without a lifeguard on duty (§ 129-3.1).
- **Penalty:** § 1-15 (§ 129-7A). Members who strike or threaten staff are removed immediately, their membership is revoked, and police are notified (§ 129-2.1D).

## General Penalty Provision — Wayne Code § 1-15

Any section that says "penalties stated in Chapter 1, Section 1-15," or that has no penalty of its own, falls under these limits.

| Provision | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| § 1-15.1A Maximum | Up to 90 days in jail, a fine up to $2,000, or up to 90 days community service, or any combination |
| § 1-15.1B Solid waste max | Up to $10,000 for unlawful solid waste disposal |
| § 1-15.2A Minimum | Council may set a minimum fine of up to $100 for a particular section |
| § 1-15.2B Solid waste min | $2,500 minimum for unlawful solid waste disposal |
| § 1-15.4 Repeat offenders | A repeat of the same violation within one year, after the earlier fine was paid, carries an additional fine |
| § 1-15.6 Housing and zoning | A fine over $1,250 to an owner requires a 30-day period to cure first |
| § 1-15.7 Separate violations | Each day is a separate violation **only** where the specific section says so |
| § 1-15.8 Judge's discretion | The judge may impose a lesser, nominal or no penalty |

**Chapter-specific penalties that override § 1-15:** Chapter 200 traffic: up to $75 or 15 days (§ 200-3). Handicapped parking: $250 (§ 200-10.5). Hydrant parking: up to $50 or 15 days (§ 205-38B). Noise under Chapter 121: civil penalty up to $2,000 through NOV/NOPA (§ 121-11). Solicitation: $100 / $500 / $1,000 (§ 60-11). Littering and dumping: $2,500–$10,000 (§ 112-16C). Underage drinking: $250 / $350 (§ 38-34). Sidewalk snow: $100 (§ 180-25). Zoning: up to $1,250 or 90 days (§ 134-122).

## Not Verified / Not Found in the Code

**Searched for, not in the Wayne code.** Use state law or another section instead.

- **Curfew:** No municipal juvenile curfew ordinance.
- **Loitering:** No loitering ordinance.
- **Panhandling:** No panhandling or begging ordinance.
- **Fireworks (general):** No townwide fireworks ordinance. Wayne regulates fireworks only in parks (§ 129-20B(2)) and indoors at businesses (§ 14-23). Use N.J.S.A. 21:3-1 et seq.
- **Fire lanes:** No standalone fire-lane ordinance. Fire zones on private lots are enforceable only where that property's § 200-35 schedule covers them. Otherwise use N.J.S.A. 39:4-138 or refer to the Fire Bureau (Uniform Fire Code).
- **Skateboards and scooters in the street:** The only rule is in parks (§ 129-20A(23)). E-scooters are covered under § 202-5B.
- **Public cannabis use:** No municipal ordinance found. Use state law.
- **Residential RV and trailer parking on the street:** No street-specific RV ban found beyond the 2–6 a.m. overnight rule (§ 200-5B). Driveway rules are in § 134-67.1.
